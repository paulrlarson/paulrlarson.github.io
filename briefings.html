How Each Briefing Is Built

An evolving, hybrid human-machine workflow for source-traceable conflict analysis.

Standard conflict reporting forces a trade-off between speed and rigor: English wire services miss localized unrest, black-box AI summaries mix unverified claims with partisan media framing, and participatory AI consultation tools require party consent rarely available in active crises. These challenges are overcome by using a hybrid human-machine workflow to deliver fast, source-traceable conflict intelligence from open multi-language records:

  • Localized visibility without party participation: Automated multi-language collection captures regional reporting, official statements, and international monitoring data when polling or mediated dialogue is impossible, logging every event claim by claim with source-confidence ratings and a numbered Substack source log.
  • Multi-perspective drivers over partisan headlines: Structured actor mapping compares each faction's stated positions against dated actions, underlying interests, and domestic constraints from multiple vantage points to explain why parties move, not just what happened.
  • Practitioner reasoning on demand: Sequenced, tested prompts mirror a conflict specialist's thought process rather than a passive resource library, using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over stage-tagged scholarship and historical cases across bargaining and game theory, conflict resolution, political economy, civil-military relations, contentious politics, and stabilization, supplemented by targeted searches for case-specific academic research.
  • Stress-tested scenarios and early-warning signposts: Historical case matching and red-team hypothesis testing expose hidden spoilers, commitment problems, and realistic negotiation space, or Zone of Possible Agreement (ZOPA), translating complex dynamics into specific "What to Watch" actions, resource shifts, and deadlines.
